The World

The Age of Wildmarks

The cities, kingdoms and great forges of the old continent built their power on Marks: ancient seals that bound weapons, walls and tools to the unseen currents of the world. When the great powers tried to unify every Mark beneath a single master seal, the bindings did not hold. They broke.

  1. I

    The Marks

    Worked into steel and stone, a Mark sharpened blades, hardened armor and guarded gates. Each region kept its own tradition, and the continent grew dependent on systems that could not speak to one another. Access to Mark resources became a power race.

  2. II

    The Arkenmark

    An alliance of the great powers, the Concord, forged one seal to govern the rest and end the Mark wars. It was meant to balance the continent's currents and reinforce what was failing. Instead, it severed every binding that kept the old Marks in check.

  3. III

    The Markfall

    Ownerless power seeped into soil and water, gathered in mines and changed the creatures that lived near it. Roads failed, cities fell silent, whole regions became too dangerous to hold. Two hundred years later, the world is still rebuilding on top of the ruins.

Can power truly be controlled?

The old world said yes. It forged the Arkenmark. And made the continent wild.

Gameplay

You are not the chosen one.

There is no prophecy waiting for you. You are an adventurer making a living in dangerous country: hunting, trading, recovering what the old world left behind, and earning friends and enemies along the way.

Hunt Wild Creatures

Concentrated Mark power changes what lives near it. Track creatures warped by the Markfall and bring down the ones worth the risk.

Wildmarked Gear

When ownerless power binds to equipment, it creates an item no smith can reproduce. Wildmarked gear only comes from the hunt, is never crafted, and is always tradable.

Markfall Relics

Ruins, waystones and broken columns still hold old-world remains. Recovering them is dangerous, and someone always pays well.

Trade and Reputation

No social score, no fellowship bar. Your name is whatever your deals, your parties and your rivals remember about you.

Parties and Guilds

Form up for the dangerous grounds. Combat is resolved on the server, so what you see is what actually happened.

A Seamless Continent

The world streams around you in interest windows. Neighbours stay visible as you move, and regions join without a loading screen between them.

Wildmarked Equipment

Gear with a history.

Three ascending grades of Wildmarked equipment are planned. The first playable slice exposes only the lowest, at a deliberately rare rate. Ordinary places stay restrained; the glow is reserved for dense Wild areas, old structures and Wildmarked gear itself.

Technology

Built on WildNet, our own engine.

Wildmarks runs on WildNet, a backend and network engine we build in-house, inspired by SpacetimeDB. The server is authoritative: identity comes from the connection, never from the client, and durable state changes only through transactional reducers written to an append-only commit log. Clients subscribe to standing queries over WebSocket and receive exactly the slice of the world they can see.

High-frequency state such as position is ephemeral and streamed at a steady cadence. Loot, inventory and progress are durable and survive a crash or a restart. Two planes, one doctrine.

WildNetRust is the engine's Rust rewrite: the same byte-compatible wire protocol, now with sharded hosts, live player handover between shards, signed write-ahead-log replication, and a Rust-native game load generator that measures fan-out latency against service-level targets. Every slice ships with automated tests before the next one begins.

Studio

A small studio, a deliberate process.

Wildmarks is made by an independent studio. The developer directs; AI agents write the code. Both the engine and the game are built with Claude Code in an orchestrated workflow: specifications first, every change reviewed, quality gates on every commit. Purchased assets never ship raw, performance claims require measurements, and nothing is marked done without green tests.
